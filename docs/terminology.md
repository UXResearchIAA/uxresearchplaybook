# UX Research Playbook — Terminology Guide

Decisions made during the August 2026 content accessibility audit.
Use this guide when writing or reviewing content for the playbook.

---

## Core terms

### research question
Use **research question** consistently. Do not alternate with "research objective," "research goal," or "research aim" unless the distinction is intentional.

- A *research question* asks what you need to learn: "Why do users abandon the checkout flow?"
- A *research objective* is acceptable when referring to a broader goal that contains multiple questions, but prefer "research question" in most SOP contexts.
- Never use "learning goal" or "knowledge objective" as a substitute.

### participant
Use **participant** consistently. Do not alternate with "user," "subject," or "respondent" within a single method page.

- "Recruit 6 participants" — not "6 users" or "6 subjects"
- "Respondent" is acceptable in a survey context only, where it has specific meaning.
- "User" is appropriate in product and design contexts ("users of the product"), not in research participant contexts.

### method
Use **method** as the primary term. Avoid unnecessary alternation with "technique," "approach," or "methodology."

- "Choose a method" — not "make a methodological selection"
- "methodology" refers to the study of methods (e.g., the CI methodology) — it is not a synonym for "method"
- "technique" is acceptable for specific sub-components of a method (e.g., "think-aloud technique") but not as a synonym for the method itself

### attitudinal / behavioral
These are the canonical terms for the two axes of the research landscape.

- **Attitudinal**: what people say they think, feel, believe, or prefer
- **Behavioral**: what people actually do or how they behave

Do not replace these with simpler alternatives. Explain them on first use if needed.

### qualitative / quantitative
Use consistently as the canonical scale terms. Do not replace with "small sample" or "survey" as generic substitutes.

## Terminology for research activities

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| analyze the data | conduct an analysis of the data |
| write a research question | formulate a research question |
| choose a method | make a methodological selection |
| ask participants to describe | participants should be asked to describe |
| recruit participants | participant recruitment should be undertaken |
| define your research question | define the knowledge objectives of the study |

## Terms with specific meanings — do not simplify

| Term | Meaning — do not replace |
|---|---|
| affinity diagramming | A bottom-up method for grouping observations into themes |
| thematic coding | Applying labels to transcript segments by meaning |
| synthesis | Converting coded themes or observations into findings and insights |
| think-aloud protocol | Having participants speak their thoughts during a task |
| severity rating | Classifying usability problems by impact (critical / moderate / minor) |
| screener | A short survey used to qualify or disqualify potential participants |
| SOP | Standard Operating Procedure — the step-by-step workflow for a method |
| design opportunity | A finding reframed as a direction for design exploration |
| workaround | A solution users build around a broken or inadequate process |

## Writing principles applied in this audit

1. **Active voice where the actor is known.**
   "The researcher asks participants to reflect" — not "participants are asked to reflect."

2. **No unnecessary nominalizations.**
   "Analyze" — not "conduct an analysis."
   "Draw out stories" — not "focus on eliciting stories."

3. **No idioms that depend on cultural fluency.**
   "Follow unexpected directions" — not "follow unexpected threads."

4. **Remove hedging that adds no information.**
   "NN/g recommends" — not "a well-known NN/g heuristic recommends."

5. **Prefer concrete verbs.**
   "Cannot produce actionable findings" — not "cannot be synthesized into actionable findings."

6. **Avoid jargon stacking.**
   "Measurable data across a large sample" — not "measurable, statistically tractable data."
